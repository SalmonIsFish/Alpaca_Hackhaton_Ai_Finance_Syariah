Stock ownership is generally permissible in Islam, viewed as a form of equity partnership (_Musharakah_). You are buying a partial, tangible stake in a company’s assets and profits rather than just a piece of paper. To be _Halal_ (lawful), investments must strictly avoid impermissible industries, unethical debt structures, and speculative behavior.

The foundational rules governing equity and stock ownership include: 

1. Underlying Business Legitimacy

A company's primary operations must be ethical and compliant with Islamic law (_Shariah_)

**Prohibited Sectors:** Investments in alcohol, pork, gambling, adult entertainment, conventional banking/insurance, and companies manufacturing weapons intended for unethical use are strictly forbidden. 

**Mixed Operations Tolerance:** If a company's primary business is permissible but generates a small amount of non-compliant income, Shariah scholars allow it, provided the non-compliant revenue remains below an acceptable threshold (typically 5%) and is "purified" by giving that portion to charity.

2. Debt and Financial Ratios

Conventional companies often use interest-bearing debt (_Riba_). To own an equity, the debt must not exceed established, safe thresholds.

**Debt to Equity Limit:** The total interest-bearing debt of the company must not be dominant; it is commonly capped at 33% of the company's total assets or market capitalization.

**Liquid Asset Limit:** A large percentage of the company's value should ideally be in tangible or illiquid assets rather than just cash and cash-equivalents (which generate interest).

3. Risk and Profit Sharing

In Islamic finance, profit is inherently tied to risk. 

- **No Guaranteed Returns:** A company cannot offer a fixed, guaranteed rate of return on shares, as this constitutes _Riba_ (usury).
- **Proportional Loss:** If the company goes into a loss, shareholders must be prepared to bear those losses proportionally to their equity stake

4. Method of Trading and Possession

Stock trading must resemble a transparent transaction rather than a casino.

- **Selling Before Possession:** You must officially own the risk of the shares before you can sell them onward.
- **Prohibited Practices:** Practices like short-selling (selling a stock you do not own) and day trading using extreme margin/leverage are generally prohibited due to the elements of excessive uncertainty (_Gharar_) and gambling (_Maysir_)

5. Obligation of Zakat

If you own stocks, you are liable to pay _Zakat_ (almsgiving) on them annually.
