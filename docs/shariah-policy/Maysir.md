**Maysir** (also known as _qimar_) is the Arabic term for gambling or any game of chance where wealth is acquired through mere luck or speculation rather than productive labor or trade. It is strictly prohibited (_haram_) in Islam because it promotes greed, damages social trust, and relies on taking wealth from others without providing fair compensation or value.

Core Reasons for Prohibition

The prohibition of _maysir_ is rooted in the Quran (such as Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:90), where it is classified alongside intoxicants as an abomination of Satan. Islam outlaws it for several ethical and socioeconomic reasons.

- Zero-Sum Transfers:Wealth is simply transferred from the loser to the winner without generating any new economic value or productive output.
- Social Harmony:Gambling breeds resentment, hostility, and addiction, which can destroy personal discipline and family stability.
- Economic Justice: It creates wealth based on wishful hope rather than fair exchange, work, or shared business risk

Examples of Maysir

While traditional gambling (casinos, sports betting, and lotteries) is the most obvious form, the concept extends into various modern practices:

- **Conventional Insurance:** Traditional life and property insurance are heavily debated because the financial payout relies on unpredictable chance (e.g., whether or not an accident or death occurs).
- **Financial Derivatives:** Pure speculation on options, futures, and naked short selling involves excessive and artificial risk.
- **Hoarding and Pure Speculation:** Business transactions where monetary gains are derived entirely from conjecture or luck.

Maysir vs. Legitimate Business

In Islamic commercial law, there is a clear distinction between prohibited _maysir_ (gambling) and lawful risk-taking (_mukhatarah_). Ordinary commercial risks—such as investing in a business or engaging in standard trade—are permissible because they involve effort, real value creation, and the mutual exchange of goods or services.
