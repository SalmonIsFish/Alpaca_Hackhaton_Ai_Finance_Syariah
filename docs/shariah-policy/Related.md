Islamic finance uses business screening to ensure capital promotes ethical practices, genuine wealth creation, and social welfare. Excluded sectors conflict with core Shariah principles—such as prohibitions against usury (_riba_), uncertainty (_gharar_), gambling (_maysir_), and societal harm.

Why Specific Sectors Are Excluded

The logic for excluding certain industries rests on religious doctrines, moral frameworks, and economic utility.

- **Conventional Banking & Insurance (Riba):** Excluded because they are primarily built on interest-based lending, time-value of money arbitrage, and speculation. Islamic finance requires risk-sharing and asset-backed transactions. https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/what-islamic-finance-how-different-from-traditional-models-lendsqr-cfvxf/

- **Gambling & Casinos (Maysir):** Excluded because wealth is generated through chance rather than productive economic effort, promoting unearned enrichment and destructive speculation. https://aims.education/principles-of-islamic-banking/

- **Alcohol & Pork (Non-Halal):** Excluded because the consumption of these items is explicitly prohibited (haram) in Shariah due to their physical, moral, and social harms. https://fusang.co/news/islamic-finance-concepts

- **Tobacco & Recreational Drugs:** Excluded because they are inherently addictive and physically harmful. Islamic law mandates the preservation of life and health. https://musaffa.com/screening-methodology

- **Adult Entertainment & Pornography:** Excluded as they degrade human dignity, contradict fundamental moral values, and commercialize illicit acts. https://aims.education/principles-of-islamic-banking/

- **Weapons & Defense:** Excluded because manufacturing weapons of mass destruction or items that cause indiscriminate harm promotes violence and societal destruction.

How the Screening Works in Practice

Because many modern, large-scale companies are involved in diverse operations, regulatory bodies (such as the [Shariah Advisory Council](https://www.sc.com.my/development/icm/shariah-compliant-securities/shariah-compliant-securities-screening-methodology) in Malaysia) apply standardized thresholds:  https://www.stashaway.my/r/understand-shariah-investing-malaysia . https://my.bursamalaysia.com/learn/knowledge/explorer/shariah-justification-securities-commission-malaysia-benchmark . https://www.instituteofhalalinvesting.org/single-post/shariah-screening-process-how-companies-qualify-for-halal-investment

1. **Qualitative Review:** Evaluates a company's core operations. If the primary line of business is in a prohibited sector (e.g., a casino operator), the company is immediately rejected. 

2. **Quantitative Benchmarks:** If a company operates in a permissible sector but generates incidental revenue from prohibited activities, it may still be considered compliant if that revenue falls below specific tolerance limits. In places like Malaysia, a widely used threshold dictates that prohibited-source revenues must not exceed 5% or 20% of the company's total revenue, depending on the nature of the activity.
