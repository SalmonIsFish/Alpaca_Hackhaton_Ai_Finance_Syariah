## Citations Ready for Incorporation

### 1. IIFA Resolution 224 (Hedging Principles)

**Current Status in fiqh-primary-sources.md:** Cited by name; full text not provided

**Stronger Citation Now Available:**
- **URL:** [International Islamic Fiqh Academy Resolution No. 224 (8/23)](https://iifa-aifi.org/en/6235.html)
- **Full Title:** On Hedging in Financial Transactions: Principles and Rules
- **Session:** 23rd session, al-Madinah al-Munawarah, October 28 – November 1, 2018

**Key Permissible Hedging Formulas** (now verifiable from primary source):
1. Cooperative hedging based on takaful insurance
2. Parallel contractual hedging (same terms as original contract)

**Requirements for Permissible Hedging** (now with direct quotes):
- "Hedging formulas should not involve or pave the way to Riba"
- "It should not contain gharar"
- "The real objective of hedging instruments should be the preservation of wealth and not the speculation of prices"
- Must avoid guaranteed returns to managers, investors, or agents

**Recommended Update:**
Replace paraphrased references with direct quote + URL in fiqh-primary-sources.md, section on Hedging Doctrine.

### 2. IIFA Resolution 238 (Options Prohibition)

**Current Status:** Cited as "IIFA 2019, prohibits options"

**Stronger Citation Now Available:**
- **URL:** [International Islamic Fiqh Academy Resolution No. 238 (9/24)](https://iifa-aifi.org/en/5985.html)
- **Full Title:** Hedging Transactions in Islamic Financial Institutions
- **Key Prohibition Quote:**
  > "Options contracts, as currently traded in the international financial markets, are new contracts that do not fall under any of the Shariah-compliant contracts. The subject matter of options is abstract rights rather than tangible assets."

**Prohibited Hedging Methods** (per primary source):
1. Mutual currency loans
2. Structured tawaruq transactions
3. Bilateral binding commitments (forward agreements)
4. Securitized sukuk with guarantees

**Recommended Update:**
Add direct quote from Resolution 238 to fiqh-primary-sources.md to show the *exact* wording of the prohibition, not just paraphrase.

### 3. Khayar al-Shart (Conditional Options in Classical Law)

**Current Status:** Named but minimal detail on permissible conditions

**Stronger Citations Now Available:**
- **Al-Islam.org Reference:** [The Option to Include Conditions (Khayar al-Shari)](https://al-islam.org/ms/five-schools-islamic-law-muhammad-jawad-mughniyya/option-include-conditions-khayar-al-shari)
- **Islamic Law Definition:** Khiyar al-shart is "an option in a sale contract, giving one of the two parties a right to cancel the sale within a stipulated time"
- **Five Types of Khiyar Recognized:** Khiyar al-Shart, Khiyar al-Tayin, Khiyar al-Ruyah, Khiyar al-Ayb, and one additional type

**Permissible Conditions** (from primary source):
- Explicitly stipulated as a contractual condition
- Time-limited (parties set the revocation period)
- Attached to a concrete sale (not an abstract right)
- Aims to protect weaker parties from harm

**Recommended Update:**
Add the Al-Islam.org reference and the definition of all five types of khiyar to show that conditional options are a recognized Islamic legal mechanism.

### 4. Wa'd and Wa'dan (Unilateral and Bilateral Promises)

**Current Status:** Referenced as used in FX forwards; limited detail

**Stronger Citations Now Available:**
- **Academic Paper:** Alisa Ahmad et al., [The Use of Wa'Dan in Islamic Contract FX Forward: Weighting between Maslahah and Mafsadah](https://ccsenet.org/journal/index.php/ass/article/view/41755), *Asian Social Science*, 2015
- **Definition from Primary Sources:**
  - **Wa'd:** Unilateral promise by one party to buy/sell at agreed rate on future date
  - **Wa'dan:** Two unilateral promises (bilateral mutual commitment), considered more just because both parties are bound

**Modern Application:** Islamic banks use Wa'd/Wa'dan to structure derivative-like FX forwards as conditional promises rather than abstract rights sales.

**Scholarly Debate:** Some scholars argue Wa'd/Wa'dan is merely conventional product relabeling; others defend it as a genuine Shariah-compliant mechanism for derivatives.

**Recommended Update:**
Add the academic paper citation and the distinction between Wa'd (unilateral) and Wa'dan (bilateral) to the hedging structures section.

### 5. Arboun/Urbun (Earnest Money / Down Payment Sale)

**Current Status:** Named; permissible conditions not fully detailed

**Stronger Citations Now Available:**
- **AAOIFI Standard:** [SS (53) Arboun (Earnest Money)](https://aaoifi.com/ss-53-arboun-earnest-money/?lang=en)
- **Permissible Conditions** (from AAOIFI and State Bank of Pakistan implementation):
  1. Earnest money is credited toward the price if contract is confirmed
  2. Seller may forfeit earnest money if buyer fails to complete during stipulated period
  3. The forfeited amount must be part of the actual price (not a separate penalty)
  4. The subject of sale must be something that can legitimately be sold
  5. Arboun may be taken on lease contracts with similar conditions

**Recommended Update:**
Add AAOIFI SS 53 as a primary source citation and detail the permissible conditions for earnest money sales to strengthen the analogy to cash-secured puts.

### Sami Al-Suwailem: Hedging in Islamic Finance

**Current Status:** Cited by title and author; URL reference provided

**Strengthening Available:**
- **Full Reference:** Sami Al-Suwailem (2006), *Hedging in Islamic Finance*, Islamic Development Bank Institute, Occasional Paper No. 10
- **Repository:** Available at [EconPapers REPEC](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ris:irtiop:0217)
- **Alternative Sources:** SlidePlayer presentation and PDF archives of the work

**Framework from Al-Suwailem:**
1. Hedging is permissible when it fulfills Shariah objectives
2. Risk management with proper backing is not prohibited
3. Form of instrument matters less than whether it serves genuine asset protection

**Recommended Update:**
Ensure the REPEC URL and the three permissibility conditions are explicitly cited in fiqh-primary-sources.md, not just as paraphrase.
